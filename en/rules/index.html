Rules

This page describes the default rules. Card effects, hand size and special rules can be changed in the app.

Goal

Whoever gets rid of all their cards first wins the game.

Dealing

The game uses 32 cards (seven to ace in four suits). Everyone gets 5 cards, one card lies face up as the discard pile, the rest is the draw pile.

Your turn

You play a card that matches the top card in suit or rank. If you cannot, you draw a card. The starting player is chosen at random.

Card effects

Seven: the next player draws 2 cards. Eight: the next player skips. Jack: wild card, the player chooses the suit. All effects, the number of cards drawn and the special rules can be changed in the app.

In detail

A draw card may be answered with a draw card; the penalties add up. Whoever would have to skip may play a skip card and pass the skip on. The wild card may be played on any card. A wild card may be played on a wild card. You may not finish with the wild card. A drawn card may be played right away if it fits.

Mau

Whoever plays the second-to-last card has to call “Mau” (button in the top bar). Otherwise they draw 2 penalty cards.

Time per turn

You have 60 seconds per turn. When time runs out, a card is drawn or a fitting card is played for you. The clock only runs while your hand is visible.

End and deadlocked games

Whoever plays the last card wins at once. If no card is played for 30 turns per player, the game ends: whoever has the fewest cards wins; a tie has no winner.

Controls

Tap a hand card to play it; matching cards glow green. Tap the draw pile to draw. If a drawn card does not fit, end the turn with “Keep card”.

Several players on one device

After every turn the hands are hidden. Pass the device on, the next player taps the screen and sees their cards.

Computer opponents

Beginner plays mostly at random. Medium uses effect cards wisely and remembers suits. Hard tracks the cards played and wishes for suits that help it. Expert plays without mistakes, blocks opponents with few cards and plans the endgame. Computers never see other players’ hands.

Mau Mau is a game by Robert Peringer.
